PSTE: High-Speed Quadrupedal Parkour with Multi-Scale Spatiotemporal Visual Encoding

Videos Simulation

Abstract

Quadrupedal robots can run rapidly on flat ground, yet sustaining high speeds on challenging terrain remains difficult: terrain changes demand accurate perception while increasing speed leaves less time to respond. We present PSTE, a reinforcement learning framework for quadrupedal parkour using a single onboard depth camera and multi-scale spatiotemporal visual encoding. A high-frequency reactive branch captures fine local geometry for rapid responses, while a lower-frequency memory branch integrates broader terrain information and its temporal history for anticipatory adjustments. To address the declining reliability of long-range depth measurements, we design a range-dependent depth degradation model to narrow the sim-to-real camera gap. With a training scheme for high-speed locomotion, PSTE sustains speeds above 3.5 m/s on real-world terrain, including continuous stairs, elevated platforms, and gaps, averaging above 4.5 m/s on the gap segment. The framework also demonstrates applicability across quadrupedal platforms.

Method

PSTE processes depth from one camera with two recurrent visual encoders. Residual fusion combines their terrain features, which the policy uses with estimated robot states and proprioception.

Reactive branch

0.2–2 m at 50 Hz. Frequent updates capture nearby geometry for rapid motion adjustments.

Memory branch

0.2–10 m at 10 Hz. Wider depth coverage and recurrent memory help the policy anticipate upcoming terrain.

Interactive simulation

The RobotA policy starts on mixed terrain when the simulator comes into view and returns to the start after each finish. The forward command is fixed at 4.5 m/s. Timing begins at the first obstacle, after the flat run-up. Drag to orbit the camera.

Calculation details

A foot is in contact when its vertical force exceeds 0.1 N. Each pair with mismatched contact states contributes 2. The joint angle term sums squared differences between corresponding thigh and calf joints within each pair. The command-dependent scale is the absolute forward command divided by the norm of the lateral and yaw commands plus 0.001. Clip that ratio to [0.01, 1] and multiply by 0.2.
